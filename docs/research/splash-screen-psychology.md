# Does a forced splash-screen pause help?

**Decision for Yorozu:** No minimum display duration. Show the oval mark and system-matched light/dark background during genuine startup, then reveal usable UI immediately. If initialization still blocks interaction, explain real progress or show a useful loading state. Do not add a delay to make users look at the mark.

## Evidence

| Study | Method and finding | Transfer limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wang et al., *Displays* 68 (2021), 102007, [publisher](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0141938221000214) | Repeated-measures comparison of four initial mobile-app loading treatments (blank, circle, logo, logo + slogan); 90 analyzed young-adult participants. Logo outperformed blank and circle on reported emotion, time perception, preference, and tolerable wait; logo + slogan was preferred. | Compares **what appears during an existing wait**, not immediate launch versus an imposed wait. Young sample and laboratory/questionnaire measures; no retention or trust outcome. Supports branding unavoidable loading, not adding loading. |
| Buell & Norton, “The Labor Illusion,” *Management Science* 57 (2011), 1564–1579, [DOI:10.1287/mnsc.1110.1376](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.1110.1376), [author abstract](https://www.hbs.edu/faculty/Pages/item.aspx?num=40158) | Five simulated online travel/dating service experiments: visible work can raise perceived value enough that participants prefer a slower site to an instant one with identical results; perceived effort and reciprocity mediate effect. | Operational transparency shows **work being done for the user**. A static oval mark conveys no such work. Different task and value context; cannot justify a decorative pause. |
| Branaghan & Sanchez, “Feedback Preferences and Impressions of Waiting,” *Human Factors* 51 (2009), 528–538, [DOI:10.1177/0018720809345684](https://doi.org/10.1177/0018720809345684), [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19899362/) | Three experiments compared waiting-feedback displays and measured preferences, apparent duration, and wait reasonableness. Feedback design changes waiting experience. | Pertains to unavoidable waits (including 15–30-second conditions), not evidence that a faster app should wait. |
| Harrison, Yeo & Hudson, “Faster Progress Bars,” CHI 2010, [DOI:10.1145/1753326.1753556](https://doi.org/10.1145/1753326.1753556), [author PDF](https://www.chrisharrison.net/projects/progressbars2/ProgressBarsHarrison.pdf) | Controlled comparisons found visual progress-bar treatments can alter **perceived** duration while actual duration stays fixed. | Subjective-time manipulation is not improved objective speed, trust, or brand recognition; no reason to manufacture wait. |

**Platform guidance:** [Apple's Human Interface Guidelines: Launching](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/launching) say to launch instantly, replace the system launch screen quickly with the first screen, and avoid branding elements unless fixed parts of that first screen. [Loading guidance](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/loading) favors showing content early and letting people act while later content loads. Yorozu's current implementation has no timed delay ([status](../status.md), [release](../release.md)).

## Interpretation

No located primary study directly tests *an otherwise ready iOS app forced to display a brand splash for extra seconds* against immediate access on psychological benefit, repeat usage, or trust. Available positive results concern **making an unavoidable wait less unpleasant or more meaningful**. Branding benefit cannot be inferred to outweigh lost control and additional objective latency. Keep launch delay at zero; assess any branding claim only with an opt-in internal A/B test measuring task-start latency, perceived speed, and preference—not by holding everyone hostage to a timer.
